# iOS Development Highlights: September 2010

Here are some of the things from across the iOS blogosphere I found interesting this past month.

- The hottest topic: Apple revised and published their [App Store review criteria](https://developer.apple.com/appstore/resources/approval/guidelines.html) (iOS developer membership required, though [Engadget has converted it into a PDF](http://www.engadget.com/2010/09/09/apples-app-store-review-guidelines-we-dont-need-any-more-far/) for everyone to read). I like the tone and spirit of that document very much. [John Gruber also documents welcome changes to the developer license agreement](http://daringfireball.net/2010/09/app_store_guidelines) (among others, third party development tools like Flash are allowed again). Despite the relaxation of the rules, [Jesper still argues for an App-Store-independent way of installing iOS apps](http://waffle.wootest.net/2010/09/09/amateur-hour/) without the need to jailbreak your device (and I agree).
- Josh Clark, author of the awesome book [Tapworthy](https://www.amazon.com/Tapworthy-Designing-Great-iPhone-Apps/dp/1449381650/) on iPhone app design [talks about some concepts from his book in an 80-minute webcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_JobmIurqCY).
- Marco Arment [analyzed the words that are most common to 5-star and 1-star reviews](http://www.marco.org/1111087530) on the App Store. The lesson: make your app simple and amazing!
- Nice set of slides by Jim Dovey on [how to design robust and fast networking code in iOS](https://quatermain.tumblr.com/post/1137996218/i-just-finished-giving-my-first-professional). Gives an overview of Apple’s networking APIs and shows how to use Grand Central Dispatch to make them more awesome.
- Manish Patel discusses [the development costs of an iOS app](http://osxdaily.com/2010/09/07/iphone-development-costs/). Related: [a similar discussion on Stack Overflow with some good numbers from insiders](http://stackoverflow.com/questions/209170/how-much-does-it-cost-to-develop-an-iphone-application).
- Google published an [open-source library to implement OAuth](https://googlemac.blogspot.com/2010/09/oauth-sign-in-controllers-for-ios-and.html) in your app.
- A number of developers started publishing the source code of their App Store apps for us to learn – and happily, some of them even seem to profit from this move financially because more people buy their app. I think this is great. John from ManiacDev has compiled a very useful (and regularly updated) [list of open source App Store apps](http://maniacdev.com/2010/06/35-open-source-iphone-app-store-apps-updated-with-10-new-apps/).
- This one is from July but I only discovered it recently: Adrian Kosmaczewski wrote [an Objective-C wrapper for the C-based Core Text API](http://akosma.com/2010/07/08/core-text-objective-c-wrapper/) (new in iOS 3.2). And anything that makes Core Text code more readable is very welcome.

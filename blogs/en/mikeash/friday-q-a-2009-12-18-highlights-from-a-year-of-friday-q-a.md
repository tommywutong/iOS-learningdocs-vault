Friday Q&A 2009-12-18: Highlights From a Year of Friday Q&A

by [Mike Ash](https://www.mikeash.com/)

The most popular article was [Mac OS X Process Memory Statistics](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-06-19-mac-os-x-process-memory-statistics.html), probably because it was accessible and relevant enough to be of interest to the average user, plus [Daring Fireball](http://daringfireball.net) linked to it. That link drove a huge amount of traffic, and that article got well over 10,000 hits to it that weekend.

The most popular article among developers, as measured by the number of comments left on it, was [my early introduction to blocks](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2008-12-26.html) (written before I had the idea of giving each one a title), which racked up an impressive 42 comments (so far).

Blocks remained a popular topic, with [Practical Blocks](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-08-14-practical-blocks.html) coming once blocks were mature enough to use them in real code.

Blocks are the natural ally of Grand Central Dispatch, which I covered in a four-part series, talking about [Basics and Dispatch Queues](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-08-28-intro-to-grand-central-dispatch-part-i-basics-and-dispatch-queues.html), [Multi-Core Performance](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-09-04-intro-to-grand-central-dispatch-part-ii-multi-core-performance.html), [Dispatch Sources](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-09-11-intro-to-grand-central-dispatch-part-iii-dispatch-sources.html), [Odds and Ends](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-09-18-intro-to-grand-central-dispatch-part-iv-odds-and-ends.html), and finally capping it all off with a [Practicum](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-09-25-gcd-practicum.html).

I did more than just talk about blocks. I did a [three](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-03-13-intro-to-the-objective-c-runtime.html)-[part](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-03-20-objective-c-messaging.html) [series](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-03-27-objective-c-message-forwarding.html) on the Objective-C runtime, [built an absolutely insane message forwarding system with LLVM](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-04-24-code-generation-with-llvm-part-2-fast-objective-c-forwarding.html), and [created a nutty blocks-based generator framework](https://www.mikeash.com/pyblog/friday-qa-2009-10-30-generators-in-objective-c.html).

There are, of course, a lot more posts. I very nearly succeeded in doing one post each week for the whole year, only missing a few weeks here and there. Thanks to my recent addition of tags support to my blog, you can [view the full list of Friday Q&A posts](http://www.mikeash.com/?page=pyblog/&tag=fridayqna).

It's been a great year, and I'm looking forward to the next one. I'll be taking next week off, but I'll be back in two weeks with another one.

And of course, no post would be complete without the solicitation for ideas. Friday Q&A is reader-driven. If you have a topic that you would like to see here, [send it in](mailto:mike@mikeash.com)!

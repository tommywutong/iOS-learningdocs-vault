[Jun 01 1986] |       ---      Introduction If your application patches any traps, it's important that the patches not depend on register `A5`. This is because you may have intercepted a trap used by a desk accessory.  If you need access to your globals within your patch, you can save `A5` (on the stack, perhaps), load `A5` from the low-memory global `CurrentA5` (this is guaranteed to be correct for your application), do whatever you have to do within your patch, then restore `A5` on the way out. Note that if you make any traps within your patch (or call the "real" version of the routine you patched), you should restore the caller's `A5` before doing so.  There are several ways of depending on `A5` within a patch that you should watch out for:   - Are you making any references to your global variables, or those of any units   that you're using, such as `thePort` from QuickDraw? These are accessed   with `A5`-relative references with negative offsets. - Are you making any inter-segment subroutine calls? These are accessed with   `A5`-relative references with positive offsets. - Are you using any system calls (either traps or "glue" routines) which will   depend on `A5` during their execution? In this case, you need to be sure that you restore   the caller's `A5` before executing the call.   To be safest, patched traps should follow the same rules as interrupt handlers.    |  | | --- | | __Note:__  In general, applications should not have to patch any traps, and risk compatibility problems if they do!
